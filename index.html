How I approach Learning & Development

From Need
to Impact

I see L&D as a simple cycle: understand the real need, define success, design the right experience, deliver it well, measure the result and improve.

Understand Define Design Deliver Measure Improve
01

Understand the Need

I do not start with a course. I start with the problem.

“What needs to change?”
ExampleA manager asks for feedback training. I check first: is it a knowledge gap, or do managers avoid hard conversations?
Business

What problem are we trying to solve?

People

What should employees know or do differently?

Evidence

How will we know the situation improved?

Check first

Is this really a learning problem, or a process, information or management problem?

02

Define Success

Before I build learning, I define what good looks like.

A clear goal makes the program useful.

I define: audience · skill gap · learning goal · success signal.

RequestManagers need feedback training.
GapFeedback is late and too general.
ObjectiveManagers can give clear and timely feedback.
SignalBetter practice score + 30-day follow-up.
03

Design the Experience

Not every problem needs e-learning. I choose the format based on the real need.

The expert knows the topic.
L&D makes it clear and useful.

I work with SMEs, managers and HR. I keep learning simple, practical and connected to real work.

Knowledge gapCourse or microlearning
Skill gapWorkshop, practice, role play
New employeeOnboarding program
Social supportBuddy program
Quick supportChecklist or job aid

06

Improve & Scale

Every program gives data about how to make it better.

Measure → Learn → Improve → Automate → Scale

A learning program is successful when something useful changes after it.

Low start rateCheck access and communication.
Low completionCheck timing, length and workload.
Low applicationAdd practice or manager support.
No business changeCheck if training was the real solution.
